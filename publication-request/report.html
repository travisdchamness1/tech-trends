Material findings

1. GlobalFoundries sets 2028 manufacturing target for 7 nm-class FD-SOI platform built for physical AI

GlobalFoundries unveiled FDX Fusion, a next-generation FD-SOI process platform designed for physical-AI workloads spanning sensing, edge inference, control and connectivity. The first generation targets seven-nanometer-class digital performance, more than 2x density improvement versus first-generation FDX, demonstrator silicon in early 2027 and initial manufacturing at its Dresden facility in 2028.

Why it matters: This is a concrete semiconductor roadmap for power-efficient edge intelligence and robotics, backed by an identified European manufacturing site and customer development activity rather than a purely research-stage process concept.

Trend: Physical AI is beginning to shape dedicated semiconductor process roadmaps that optimize compute, sensing, connectivity and power efficiency together.

  • GF unveils roadmap to deliver the world's most advanced FD-SOI platform for Physical AI (primary-company-announcement, 2026-10-09)

2. SpaceX moves Starlink Mobile toward full-carrier capability with nationwide 800 MHz spectrum agreement

SpaceX announced an agreement to acquire up to 14 MHz of paired nationwide 800 MHz low-band spectrum, pending FCC approval. The company says the spectrum closes a key technical gap for Starlink Mobile and will support a hybrid architecture combining satellite-to-mobile service with terrestrial deployment.

Why it matters: Owning low-band terrestrial-compatible spectrum materially changes Starlink Mobile's infrastructure position by improving range and in-building coverage while giving SpaceX a path beyond supplemental satellite connectivity toward a broader mobile-network architecture.

Trend: Satellite direct-to-device networks are converging with terrestrial carrier infrastructure rather than remaining a separate coverage layer.

  • Building the world's most advanced mobile network (primary-company-announcement, 2026-10-08)

3. NASA and DOE formalize end-to-end collaboration on space nuclear power and propulsion

NASA and the U.S. Department of Energy signed a memorandum of understanding covering advanced research, fuel production, testing, launch integration and operations for space nuclear systems. NASA tied the framework to Space Reactor-1 Freedom, planned for 2028, and the lunar surface reactor program targeted for 2030.

Why it matters: The agreement establishes an integrated government development framework across the full nuclear-space supply chain, reducing organizational fragmentation around fuel, reactors, testing and mission integration.

Trend: Deep-space infrastructure is shifting toward programmatic nuclear power and propulsion rather than relying only on solar and chemical systems.

  • NASA, Energy Department Advance New Era of Nuclear-Powered Exploration (primary-government-announcement, 2026-10-08)

4. FDA approves first adjuvant immunotherapy regimen for stage III dMMR colon cancer

Roche announced FDA approval of Tecentriq and Tecentriq Hybreza with fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin chemotherapy after surgery for stage III dMMR colon cancer. In the Phase III ATOMIC study, the regimen reduced the risk of disease recurrence or death by 50% versus chemotherapy alone, with 36-month disease-free survival of 86% versus 76%.

Why it matters: The approval establishes the first immunotherapy-based adjuvant regimen in this molecularly defined stage III colon-cancer population and introduces biomarker-specific treatment into a setting that had not previously differentiated standard adjuvant therapy by mismatch-repair status.

Trend: Oncology commercialization is continuing to move treatment selection earlier in disease course and more tightly around tumor biology.

  • FDA approves Roche's Tecentriq in combination with a fluoropyrimidine and oxaliplatin for the adjuvant treatment of a certain type of stage III colon cancer (primary-company-announcement, 2026-10-09)

5. FDA approves once-monthly subcutaneous olanzapine without post-injection monitoring

Teva announced FDA approval of WELTRUZA, a once-monthly subcutaneous extended-release olanzapine for adults with schizophrenia. The product requires no loading dose, oral supplementation or post-injection monitoring and uses Medincell's SteadyTeq controlled-release technology.

Why it matters: The approval converts a widely used daily antipsychotic into a long-acting formulation with fewer administration burdens, addressing adherence and monitoring barriers that have limited long-acting olanzapine use.

Trend: Long-acting drug-delivery platforms are expanding established medicines into lower-burden formulations with materially different care workflows.

  • Teva Announces U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Approval of WELTRUZA (Olanzapine) For Extended-Release Injectable Suspension (primary-company-announcement, 2026-10-09)
