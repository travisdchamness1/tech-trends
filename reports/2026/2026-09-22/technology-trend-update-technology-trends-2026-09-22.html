1. Alibaba unveils Zhenwu V900 and targets more than 20 GW of data-center capacity by 2032

Alibaba unveiled its Zhenwu V900 AI accelerator at the Apsara conference, saying it delivers three times the performance of its predecessor and can scale to clusters of as many as 500,000 units. CEO Eddie Wu also set a target for Alibaba Cloud data-center capacity to exceed 20 gigawatts by 2032, while the V900 is expected to enter mass production in early 2027.

Why it matters: The combination of a vertically developed accelerator, a stated mass-production path and a 20-GW infrastructure target is a concrete commitment to full-stack AI capacity at a scale comparable to major Western buildouts. It also increases evidence that advanced AI compute supply is diversifying geographically despite semiconductor trade constraints.

Trend: AI infrastructure competition is broadening from accelerator design into vertically integrated chip, cluster and multi-gigawatt data-center buildouts.

2. DOE selects 21 projects for $99 million of next-generation geothermal field work

The U.S. Department of Energy selected 21 projects for more than $99 million in potential funding: five field-scale enhanced-geothermal-system tests and 16 exploration-drilling projects intended to identify and characterize next-generation geothermal resources. DOE says project data will be made available through the Geothermal Data Repository.

Why it matters: Field-scale EGS testing and exploration drilling move geothermal development beyond laboratory research toward resource validation and commercial de-risking. The portfolio spreads technical learning across multiple sites while making resulting data reusable by industry.

Trend: Next-generation geothermal is moving into a broader field-validation phase aimed at reducing subsurface and development risk before commercial deployment.
