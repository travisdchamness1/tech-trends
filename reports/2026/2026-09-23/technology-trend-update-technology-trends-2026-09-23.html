1. Supermicro begins shipping NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 racks with full liquid-cooling stack

Supermicro says it is now shipping production NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 racks integrated with its Data Center Building Block Solutions and DLC-2 liquid cooling. Its deployment blueprints span 5 MW to gigawatt scale; one Scalable Unit contains 1,152 Rubin GPUs across 16 compute racks with 331 TB of HBM4.

Why it matters: This moves NVIDIA's Rubin generation from roadmap into shippable rack-scale infrastructure and couples compute, power, cooling, networking, testing, and deployment into a repeatable data-center build unit.

Trend: Frontier AI infrastructure is shifting from chip announcements toward factory-like delivery of integrated, liquid-cooled rack systems that can scale from megawatts to gigawatts.

2. XPENG puts humanoid-robot production line into operation and schedules mass production

XPENG says its robot production line is now operating, it has signed designation agreements with component suppliers, and manufacturing lines are being scheduled for production. Its humanoid robots are scheduled to enter mass production by the end of 2026, with initial deployments in XPENG stores and campuses and broader China and overseas delivery planned for 2027.

Why it matters: A functioning production line, supplier designations, and a dated mass-production target are stronger commercialization evidence than a prototype demonstration. They indicate embodied AI is beginning to acquire an automotive-style manufacturing and supply-chain structure.

Trend: Humanoid robotics is moving from prototype iteration toward organized component supply chains, dedicated production lines, and initial commercial deployment.

XPENG — First Robotics Supply Chain Partner Conference

3. U.S. Army selects domestically manufactured rare-earth magnets for million-drone-per-year SkyFoundry initiative

Vulcan Elements says the U.S. Army selected it to supply high-performance NdFeB magnets for SkyFoundry, the Army's vertically integrated small-drone initiative targeting one million drones per year at Tobyhanna Army Depot. The solicitation required a fully domestic, vertically integrated manufacturing process.

Why it matters: The award links a high-volume autonomous-systems production target to domestic critical-material manufacturing, turning supply-chain resilience from policy intent into a procurement requirement for a scaled defense manufacturing program.

Trend: Strategic autonomy manufacturing is increasingly being coupled to domestic critical-material supply chains as production targets move toward mass scale.
