Material findings

1. U.S. Energy Department commits nearly $2 billion to unlock more than 23 GW from the existing grid

The U.S. Energy Department is allocating nearly $2 billion across 31 projects in 26 states to increase usable capacity on existing transmission infrastructure. The projects are expected to unlock more than 23 GW using technologies including real-time weather sensing and power-flow controls.

Why it matters: This is a large-scale move to treat grid-enhancing technologies as near-term capacity infrastructure rather than waiting solely for new transmission construction. The program directly targets the electricity bottleneck being intensified by AI and data-center demand.

2. NASA selects PRIMA as first Probe Explorers mission and advances it to Phase B

NASA selected the PRobe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics as the first mission in its new Probe Explorers class and moved it into Phase B preliminary design and technology development. Subject to a later confirmation review, the mission has a project-cost cap of $1.2 billion excluding launch and targets a 2033 launch for a five-year mission.

Why it matters: Selection into Phase B converts a competed mission concept into a funded development program and establishes NASA's new mid-scale Probe Explorers mission class.

3. AGIBOT deploys more than 300 robots at Chimelong as its 20,000th production robot is delivered

AGIBOT and Chimelong launched the first phase of a deployment placing more than 300 robots into entertainment, education, visitor services, and hotel operations at Chimelong Spaceship Park. The launch coincided with delivery of AGIBOT's 20,000th production robot and uses dedicated connectivity, multi-robot coordination, and safety systems.

Why it matters: The combination of a 20,000-unit manufacturing milestone and a single-site deployment measured in hundreds of robots is stronger commercialization evidence than prototype demonstrations.
