Material findings

1. FDA grants accelerated approval to Ultomiris for IgA nephropathy

AstraZeneca announced U.S. accelerated approval of Ultomiris (ravulizumab) to reduce proteinuria in adults with primary IgA nephropathy at risk of progression. The I CAN Phase III interim analysis showed a 43.4% placebo-adjusted reduction in proteinuria at week 34. Whether Ultomiris slows kidney function decline has not yet been established; eGFR data at week 106 remain pending.

Why it matters: The first approved C5 inhibitor for this disease represents a completed regulatory commercialization milestone, not confirmed prevention of kidney failure.

2. Infineon and NVIDIA advance GaN power conversion for 800 VDC AI racks

Infineon extended its NVIDIA collaboration on gallium-nitride high-voltage intermediate bus converters. Specified conversion options include 800 V to 50 V, 12 V and 6 V, with switching frequencies up to 1 MHz. This extends earlier work and does not establish mass deployment or independently measured fleet-wide energy savings.

Why it matters: Rack-scale AI requires efficient, compact power conversion as electrical density rises.

Primary source — October 21, 2026

3. Teradyne Robotics and Sereact report hundreds of deployed physical-AI robots

Teradyne Robotics and Sereact formalized their partnership combining Sereact Cortex with Universal Robots arms for returns, picking and sorting. They report hundreds of dual-arm systems shipped to live customer sites in Europe and intend to expand the integration through global channels.

Why it matters: Commercial customer installations are more material than demonstrations, although independently audited throughput and unit economics remain unavailable.

Primary source — October 21, 2026

4. Agility reports over $300M conditional Digit 5 orders and plans new support center

Agility Robotics disclosed over $300 million in multi-year Digit 5 orders subject to contractual milestones and four additional customers. It plans a 50,000-square-foot Pittsburgh deployment, service and AI facility, expanding its existing footprint by about 35,000 square feet. Existing robot operations include GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada.

Why it matters: Orders are not revenue and the new site is not operational; the disclosure nevertheless indicates commercial pipeline and investment in fleet support.
